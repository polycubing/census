- `JSONDocument`: nested hash keys serialize in sorted order, so a record is defined by its content and not by whichever writer produced it. Certificates round-tripped through the coordinator's `jsonb` columns come back with keys rearranged, which would otherwise land the same answer in `data/` as a different file. Array order is untouched. Top-level keys keep the caller's readable order.
- `JSONDocument` pretty-prints: a space inside braces, after key colons, and after commas. Arrays of hashes break one element per line, and a hash breaks whenever one of its children did, so a container is never half inline and half stacked. Arrays of non-hashes stay inline, so `cells` keeps reading as a list of coordinates. Records grow about 17% and the widest line drops from 647 characters to 506, the remainder being prose inside heesch refutations.
- `script/canonicalize`: rewrites shape records in canonical form, comparing every file as parsed JSON before writing so a run that would change a record's content stops instead. Writes are atomic, since the long-running hunts read from `data/` while it sweeps.
- `.gitattributes` and `.prettierignore`: the archive is marked generated so GitHub keeps it out of language statistics, its line endings are pinned to LF so a volunteer on Windows checks out byte-identical records, and Prettier is told to leave the formatting to `script/canonicalize`.
- `JSONDocument` breaks a hash that would overflow 100 columns, so heesch certificates and the whuts.org attribution in `credits` stack instead of running long. Arrays are exempt at any width, so a nonacube's `cells` stays a line of coordinates rather than a column of digits. The widest line in the archive drops from 506 characters to 276, the remainder being single strings, which JSON cannot wrap.
- `JSONDocument` verifies its own output: every generated document is parsed back and compared to the record it was given, raising `ContentChanged` if they differ. The four record-writing scripts under `script/` have no test coverage, so the check lives where none of them can route around it. Keys are escaped by the stdlib rather than interpolated, since a key holding a quote would have ended the string and produced invalid JSON.
- `script/at_home/seed` rejects unknown options instead of reading them as N. A mistyped flag was shifted off as the cube count and died in `Integer()` with a raw backtrace. Extra positional arguments are rejected too.
- Fixed `.gitattributes` marking DRAT proofs as text. Kissat writes its binary format, and line-ending normalization inside those bytes would corrupt a refutation. A corrupt proof is worse than no proof, since it fails to check and makes a proven shape look unproven. Every other file under `data/` is ASCII and stays pinned to LF.
- `SAT::Proof` addresses a DRAT proof by the digest of its bytes, and `Kissat.solve_cube` writes one on request. A volunteer's refutation can then be named without being shipped: the digest travels back with the result while the bytes stay put, and the formula the proof refutes is rebuildable from the base CNF and the cube, so it never has to move either.
- Cube refutations now name a proof. The client always writes a DRAT proof, keeps it under its own SHA-256 so the same refutation lands on the same file, and reports the digest and size. The coordinator refuses an `unsat` that names no proof, a digest that did not come from hashing a file, or a proof of zero bytes, and its note says plainly that an accepted proof is still unchecked. This is weaker than checking a proof and much stronger than the volunteer's bare word: the digest binds them to an artifact they must still be able to produce. `--proofs DIR` chooses where they are kept.
- `SAT::DratTrim` checks a DRAT proof against the formula it claims to refute, reading its verdict from drat-trim's own `s` line rather than searching the output, so a proof carrying those bytes cannot claim its own success. A missing checker raises rather than reporting "unverified", since "we could not check" and "it is wrong" are different answers. The time limit is 300 seconds rather than drat-trim's default of 40000, because a coordinator cannot spend eleven hours discovering a stranger's proof was rubbish. Verified against the square ring's real corona-2 refutation: 3.7 million resolution steps in 31 seconds.
- `script/setup` builds the vendored drat-trim, which has no Homebrew formula. Only the coordinator needs it.
- Proof upload closes the loop on refutations. The coordinator marks a claimed proof `wanted`, and because a client polls rather than listens, the request rides the reply to its next lease. `POST /proof/:sha256` takes raw bytes under a 100 MB cap, GitHub's blob limit, above which a record says "too large to check this way" rather than nothing. Bytes that do not hash to the digest they arrived under are refused, which is the whole point of naming them by content. What is checked is the formula the coordinator rebuilds from its own base CNF and cube, never one the sender supplied, so a real proof of some other formula is caught and recorded as `refuted`.
- The client handle no longer carries the process id. Every restart used to register a stranger, which scattered credits across single-unit contributors and left the coordinator asking for a proof from an identity that would never come back. It is now generated once into `~/.polycubing-at-home/handle` and reused, so several clients on one machine are one volunteer.
- `script/at_home/moderate` bridges the two identities. `approve_display_name` and `credit_string` had been built and left unreachable, so nothing could ever set a client to `approved` and every volunteer was destined to be credited in `data/` by their opaque handle forever. The script lists names waiting, with contact and result counts, and approves or rejects by handle. Reversible, because the credit string is computed when a record is archived rather than baked in at submission.
- `script/at_home/audit` calls in the promise a refutation makes. It lists the proofs claimed but not held, the ones standing on a volunteer's word alone, and asks for one by digest or for all of them. Nothing happens immediately: a client polls rather than listens, so the request rides the reply to its next lease.
- Cube units serve their formula over HTTP. A cube unit named its CNF by filesystem path, which only worked when the client and coordinator were the same machine, so no remote volunteer could solve one at all. The lease now carries a digest instead of a path, `GET /cnf/:unit_id` streams the formula, and the client caches it by digest. A shape split into thousands of cubes therefore downloads its formula once. The coordinator's own paths are no longer disclosed to anyone.
- Cubes split themselves. A volunteer spends at most `--cube-timeout` seconds on one cube (an hour by default) and hands back anything unfinished as `exhausted` rather than grinding on it. The coordinator halves it on the lowest variable the cube does not already fix, marks the parent `split`, and queues both halves. A half that is still too hard is halved again, which is the escalation the n=9 campaign ran by hand. Soundness rests on `v` and `not v` covering every assignment, so nothing can hide in the gap: once every child is refuted the parent is settled, walking up the tree. A child coming back satisfiable settles it the other way and must never be read as progress, so a parent is settled only on verified `unsat` results, never on children merely being finished. `Kissat` gained a third answer, `:undecided`, for a solve a limit stopped.
- 9/2127 corona-3: single-solver attack abandoned on the laptop after 128.4 hours. One core of default-configuration kissat reached 432,367,293 conflicts, 4,599,092 restarts and 7,493 reductions without a verdict. The telemetry says why it was not close. Preprocessing removed three quarters of the formula's 1,610,910 variables in the first ten hours, then elimination flatlined, taking another 117 hours to remove just 8,057 more. Average learned-clause glue degraded from 3.3 to 12.5 across the run, where lower is better, while the two colo solvers on the identical formula held 5.5 (`kissat --unsat`) and 7.2 (`cadical`) at a third of the conflict rate. More conflicts turned out to be less progress. Memory was never the danger it first looked: RSS fell from 2.79 GB to 2.12 GB as reductions reclaimed clauses, and cadical's 9.7 GB oscillates between 7.7 and 10.0 GB rather than climbing. The colo pair continues. The attempt log is kept at `data/9/2127/cnf/corona3-mac-attempt.log`, gitignored, because "one core, 5.4 days, 432 million conflicts, no answer" is the evidence that motivates splitting.
- Why a sequential win would not have counted anyway: none of the three long-running solvers was writing a DRAT proof, so `s UNSATISFIABLE` would have been a solver's word with no artifact behind it. Proof output cannot simply be switched on either, since a monolithic DRAT for a 3.6 GB formula is the 71 GB file that already died once. The sequential runs can therefore report the answer but never prove it, and the only publishable route to refuting 9/2127's corona-3 is cube-and-conquer with per-cube proofs small enough to keep. That is the argument for pointing the @home splitting machinery at this shape rather than waiting on a lottery ticket.

Proof-checking specs are tagged `:checker` and skip with a pointer to `script/setup` when the binary is absent, so a fresh clone runs the rest of the suite instead of drowning. Because skipping could otherwise turn a broken build into a green run, CI asserts the binary exists before any spec runs. Only the two coordinator specs that actually invoke the checker are tagged, so the refusals that matter most (bytes not matching their digest, a proof nobody asked for) still run everywhere.

It now runs an actual query once and remembers the answer. This is why CI was red before drat-trim was ever the problem.

- The coordinator has a proof policy. `on_request`, the default, is what it did before: proofs are asked for by hand and a parent settles on claimed digests. `every` asks for each proof the moment its refutation is accepted, checks it with drat-trim on arrival, lets a parent settle only when every child's proof is checked, and rolls up again when a checked proof is the last one a parent was waiting on. The upload cap and the checker's time limit are configurable alongside it, since a campaign's proofs go to S3 rather than git and some cubes took hours to solve. `script/at_home/server --proof-policy every --max-proof-bytes N --check-timeout S`.

- One delivery of a proof now checks it against every unit that claimed it. Unit propagation alone refutes thousands of the easy cubes, and kissat writes the identical one-line DRAT proof for each, so a single digest stood for 1,100 results and each upload verified one of them while the clients re-sent the same bytes on every lease. The client is now asked for a shared digest once, and the coordinator checks the bytes against each claiming unit's own formula, since the same proof can refute several cubes and each cube is a different formula.
- drat-trim is told a proof's format instead of guessing it. It sniffs the first twelve bytes for anything non-printable, and a kissat binary proof whose first twelve bytes happen to be printable gets read as text and fails: three of the triskelion campaign's first two thousand proofs did. `DratTrim.binary?` decides from the first byte (binary DRAT begins with `a`, or `d` and a literal byte) and passes `-i` or `-I`. Text proofs still work, which matters because drat-trim's own trimmed cores are text.
- Checking moved out of the request. A delivered proof was checked inside the HTTP request, and one 727 MB proof held a server thread for many minutes while every client's request timed out behind it, and their retries uploaded the same proof again into more checks. With `--defer-checks` the coordinator stores delivered bytes and returns at once, and `script/at_home/check-proofs --loop --jobs N` verifies stored proofs in its own process, several drat-trims at a time, rolling up what passes. `Coordinator#recheck` is the same pass run once, for `stored` and `refuted` proofs after the checker is built or fixed, which both happened during the campaign.
- Fixed a truncated proof surviving forever under its digest's name. A server restart killed a write partway, leaving a 19 MB prefix of a 76 MB proof, and every later delivery of that digest kept the file because it existed. Proofs are now written under a temporary name and renamed, an existing file is kept only if its size and digest match, and the checker asks a client again for any held file whose size disagrees with the claim instead of checking it. The three "refuted" proofs of the triskelion campaign were this.
- The colo's disk filled: 885 GB at 100%, with the campaign's proofs on it twice (the coordinator's copy and the clients'), and the hard cubes still to come. Proofs now flow through the colo and live on the laptop. A client deletes its copy of a proof once the coordinator has accepted the bytes. `script/at_home/drain-proofs <host> <dir>`, run from the laptop, asks the coordinator's database for every verified proof, rsyncs them home, and removes them from the coordinator. Nothing in flight is touched.
- A client survives its solver dying. kissat failing to flush a proof to a full disk used to raise straight out of the client's loop, and ten of twelve died in one minute. The crash is now reported as an `error` verdict, which the coordinator turns into a released unit, and the client waits half a minute before the next lease since whatever killed the solver is probably still true.
- `script/at_home/hand-over` delivers every wanted proof found in a directory, whoever claimed it. A wanted proof is requested only from the client that claimed it, so when 28 clients were not relaunched their 37 proofs, 11 GB, sat wanted with no one to deliver them and the cubes they refuted could never close.
- The checker runs continuously. `check-proofs --loop` worked through one snapshot at a time, and a pass could not end until its biggest proof did, so a proof stored after the snapshot waited hours behind one hour-long check. `Coordinator#check_continuously` keeps a pile sorted smallest first, refills it from the database every few seconds while its workers run, and reports each proof as it finishes.
- `docs/at-home-flow.md`: how work and artifacts should move between the hub, volunteers, and checkers so that no machine is ground to a halt, written after the first campaign day found every gap by hitting it. Backpressure at the faucet, volunteer self-limits, checkers that pull, declared limits, in that order.
- PLAN gains a section on stories: write-ups of special cases, led by the four noses (9/8203, 8214, 8219, 8220, one base shape with a domino pointing four ways, two refuted and two open), whose investigation on 2026-10-08 corrected a wrong intuition about plugging a pocket with a verified witness and a rotatable model.
- Backpressure at the faucet, the first piece of `docs/at-home-flow.md`. The coordinator's basin is the bytes of proof it holds (`stored`) or expects (`wanted`). Above `--basin-high-gb` it leases nothing, below `--basin-low-gb` it resumes, and under `--disk-floor-gb` free on the proof directory's disk it leases nothing regardless. A closed faucet answers `/lease` with `retry_after`, which the client waits out, even under `--once`, because a full basin is not an empty queue. `status` reports the basin. With this, any number of clients can run and the hub throttles them to the checker's pace, which is what the first campaign day did by hand.
- The basin counts only proofs on the hub's own disk. Counting wanted proofs too let seven that no client could ever deliver hold the faucet shut for an hour with nothing stored. `script/at_home/reopen-undelivered` withdraws a refutation whose proof was wanted longer than an hour and never came, marking it unverified and queuing its cube again for a fresh solve, since a claim with no proof behind it is worth nothing to a theorem.
- Clients have a disk floor of their own, `--disk-floor-gb`: no new work while their own disk is under it. The hub cannot know a volunteer's limits for it.
- The per-IP throttle is configurable (`--throttle-limit`, `AT_HOME_THROTTLE_LIMIT`). Fifty clients on the coordinator's own machine are one IP and were being throttled at 120 requests a minute.
- `Rotation#images`, `#angle`, `#axis`: the 24 rotations readable by a person, not only by the code. `script/rotations` prints them as a table (number, where each new coordinate comes from, and the turn: identity, 90° or 180° about a face axis, 180° about an edge, 120° about a corner). README carries the table under "The data", since a certificate's `rotation 13` meant nothing to anyone without the source, and a spec keeps the README copy equal to the script's output.
- Tiling chunks are solid. `Assembly` drew a torus certificate as its eight lattice translates with coefficients 0 or 1, which is a solid double brick when the lattice is a brick and a scaffold with gaps when it is skewed: 7/206's page showed two groups of copies with an empty row between them. Now, when the eight translates do not fill their bounding box, the window grows by the shape's reach on every side and every copy that fits inside is drawn, and the translates' box is asserted to be covered exactly once. Brick lattices are untouched, so box pages and brick-lattice torus pages render as before. The `tiling.obj` files beside the records need regenerating with `script/gallery`.
- 9/8219 carries `certificate.checked`: its published 7.3 GB core proof, pulled back from S3 and decompressed like a stranger would, is drat-trim `s VERIFIED` in 24,452 s on the laptop (the colo run had hit drat-trim's 40,000 s timeout under campaign load). Every refutation-backed record now has the block.
- Tiling chunks leave git. Solid chunks made the 56,449 `tiling.obj` files about 22 GB, so they are gitignored and `script/publish-meshes` mirrors them to `s3://polycubes/public/meshes/<n>/<i>/`, where the site links them. `model.stl` and the corona meshes stay in git, and the gallery now writes corona meshes for the triskelion pair too.
- A proof over the hub's cap splits its cube instead of stalling it. Eight cubes on the colo produced proofs of 2.4 to 3.0 GB, over the 2 GiB cap, and the server's 413 left their claims `wanted`: every client re-uploaded its file on every lease and was refused again (one client logged 568 refusals), `reopen-undelivered` re-queued the cubes after an hour, the re-solves produced new oversized files under new digests, and 16 GB of them pushed the colo's disk under the floor, which closed the faucet. Now a claim whose proof is over the cap is marked `too_large` the moment it is submitted, its cube is split the way a cube too hard to solve is, and the worker is told to discard the file. The lease reply carries `discard_proofs` for claims made before the cap applied, so a restarted hub repairs the backlog on the next lease. The proof route no longer answers 413 for the same reason.
- Hand-over is room-aware. The lease reply asks for proofs smallest first and only as far as the hub's free disk above its floor allows, counting each upload twice because the web server buffers it before the hub stores it. Without a floor it asks for everything, as before.
- A client no longer dies when another client sharing its proof directory deleted the same proof first. Twenty colo clients shared `~/client-proofs`, the same one-line proof was accepted from several of them within a second, and seventeen crashed on `File.delete` at 05:10 UTC on 2026-10-08. Three clients carried the campaign for the next sixteen hours before anyone noticed.
