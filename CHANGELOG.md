- `JSONDocument`: nested hash keys serialize in sorted order, so a record is defined by its content and not by whichever writer produced it. Certificates round-tripped through the coordinator's `jsonb` columns come back with keys rearranged, which would otherwise land the same answer in `data/` as a different file. Array order is untouched. Top-level keys keep the caller's readable order.
- `JSONDocument` pretty-prints: a space inside braces, after key colons, and after commas. Arrays of hashes break one element per line, and a hash breaks whenever one of its children did, so a container is never half inline and half stacked. Arrays of non-hashes stay inline, so `cells` keeps reading as a list of coordinates. Records grow about 17% and the widest line drops from 647 characters to 506, the remainder being prose inside heesch refutations.
- `script/canonicalize`: rewrites shape records in canonical form, comparing every file as parsed JSON before writing so a run that would change a record's content stops instead. Writes are atomic, since the long-running hunts read from `data/` while it sweeps.
- `.gitattributes` and `.prettierignore`: the archive is marked generated so GitHub keeps it out of language statistics, its line endings are pinned to LF so a volunteer on Windows checks out byte-identical records, and Prettier is told to leave the formatting to `script/canonicalize`.
- `JSONDocument` breaks a hash that would overflow 100 columns, so heesch certificates and the whuts.org attribution in `credits` stack instead of running long. Arrays are exempt at any width, so a nonacube's `cells` stays a line of coordinates rather than a column of digits. The widest line in the archive drops from 506 characters to 276, the remainder being single strings, which JSON cannot wrap.
- `JSONDocument` verifies its own output: every generated document is parsed back and compared to the record it was given, raising `ContentChanged` if they differ. The four record-writing scripts under `script/` have no test coverage, so the check lives where none of them can route around it. Keys are escaped by the stdlib rather than interpolated, since a key holding a quote would have ended the string and produced invalid JSON.
- `script/at_home/seed` rejects unknown options instead of reading them as N. A mistyped flag was shifted off as the cube count and died in `Integer()` with a raw backtrace. Extra positional arguments are rejected too.
- Fixed `.gitattributes` marking DRAT proofs as text. Kissat writes its binary format, and line-ending normalization inside those bytes would corrupt a refutation. A corrupt proof is worse than no proof, since it fails to check and makes a proven shape look unproven. Every other file under `data/` is ASCII and stays pinned to LF.
- `SAT::Proof` addresses a DRAT proof by the digest of its bytes, and `Kissat.solve_cube` writes one on request. A volunteer's refutation can then be named without being shipped: the digest travels back with the result while the bytes stay put, and the formula the proof refutes is rebuildable from the base CNF and the cube, so it never has to move either.
- Cube refutations now name a proof. The client always writes a DRAT proof, keeps it under its own SHA-256 so the same refutation lands on the same file, and reports the digest and size. The coordinator refuses an `unsat` that names no proof, a digest that did not come from hashing a file, or a proof of zero bytes, and its note says plainly that an accepted proof is still unchecked. This is weaker than checking a proof and much stronger than the volunteer's bare word: the digest binds them to an artifact they must still be able to produce. `--proofs DIR` chooses where they are kept.
- `SAT::DratTrim` checks a DRAT proof against the formula it claims to refute, reading its verdict from drat-trim's own `s` line rather than searching the output, so a proof carrying those bytes cannot claim its own success. A missing checker raises rather than reporting "unverified", since "we could not check" and "it is wrong" are different answers. The time limit is 300 seconds rather than drat-trim's default of 40000, because a coordinator cannot spend eleven hours discovering a stranger's proof was rubbish. Verified against the square ring's real corona-2 refutation: 3.7 million resolution steps in 31 seconds.
- `script/setup` builds the vendored drat-trim, which has no Homebrew formula. Only the coordinator needs it.
- Proof upload closes the loop on refutations. The coordinator marks a claimed proof `wanted`, and because a client polls rather than listens, the request rides the reply to its next lease. `POST /proof/:sha256` takes raw bytes under a 100 MB cap, GitHub's blob limit, above which a record says "too large to check this way" rather than nothing. Bytes that do not hash to the digest they arrived under are refused, which is the whole point of naming them by content. What is checked is the formula the coordinator rebuilds from its own base CNF and cube, never one the sender supplied, so a real proof of some other formula is caught and recorded as `refuted`.
- The client handle no longer carries the process id. Every restart used to register a stranger, which scattered credits across single-unit contributors and left the coordinator asking for a proof from an identity that would never come back. It is now generated once into `~/.polycubing-at-home/handle` and reused, so several clients on one machine are one volunteer.
- `script/at_home/moderate` bridges the two identities. `approve_display_name` and `credit_string` had been built and left unreachable, so nothing could ever set a client to `approved` and every volunteer was destined to be credited in `data/` by their opaque handle forever. The script lists names waiting, with contact and result counts, and approves or rejects by handle. Reversible, because the credit string is computed when a record is archived rather than baked in at submission.
- `script/at_home/audit` calls in the promise a refutation makes. It lists the proofs claimed but not held, the ones standing on a volunteer's word alone, and asks for one by digest or for all of them. Nothing happens immediately: a client polls rather than listens, so the request rides the reply to its next lease.
- Cube units serve their formula over HTTP. A cube unit named its CNF by filesystem path, which only worked when the client and coordinator were the same machine, so no remote volunteer could solve one at all. The lease now carries a digest instead of a path, `GET /cnf/:unit_id` streams the formula, and the client caches it by digest. A shape split into thousands of cubes therefore downloads its formula once. The coordinator's own paths are no longer disclosed to anyone.
- Cubes split themselves. A volunteer spends at most `--cube-timeout` seconds on one cube (an hour by default) and hands back anything unfinished as `exhausted` rather than grinding on it. The coordinator halves it on the lowest variable the cube does not already fix, marks the parent `split`, and queues both halves. A half that is still too hard is halved again, which is the escalation the n=9 campaign ran by hand. Soundness rests on `v` and `not v` covering every assignment, so nothing can hide in the gap: once every child is refuted the parent is settled, walking up the tree. A child coming back satisfiable settles it the other way and must never be read as progress, so a parent is settled only on verified `unsat` results, never on children merely being finished. `Kissat` gained a third answer, `:undecided`, for a solve a limit stopped.
- 9/2127 corona-3: single-solver attack abandoned on the laptop after 128.4 hours. One core of default-configuration kissat reached 432,367,293 conflicts, 4,599,092 restarts and 7,493 reductions without a verdict. The telemetry says why it was not close. Preprocessing removed three quarters of the formula's 1,610,910 variables in the first ten hours, then elimination flatlined, taking another 117 hours to remove just 8,057 more. Average learned-clause glue degraded from 3.3 to 12.5 across the run, where lower is better, while the two colo solvers on the identical formula held 5.5 (`kissat --unsat`) and 7.2 (`cadical`) at a third of the conflict rate. More conflicts turned out to be less progress. Memory was never the danger it first looked: RSS fell from 2.79 GB to 2.12 GB as reductions reclaimed clauses, and cadical's 9.7 GB oscillates between 7.7 and 10.0 GB rather than climbing. The colo pair continues. The attempt log is kept at `data/9/2127/cnf/corona3-mac-attempt.log`, gitignored, because "one core, 5.4 days, 432 million conflicts, no answer" is the evidence that motivates splitting.
- Why a sequential win would not have counted anyway: none of the three long-running solvers was writing a DRAT proof, so `s UNSATISFIABLE` would have been a solver's word with no artifact behind it. Proof output cannot simply be switched on either, since a monolithic DRAT for a 3.6 GB formula is the 71 GB file that already died once. The sequential runs can therefore report the answer but never prove it, and the only publishable route to refuting 9/2127's corona-3 is cube-and-conquer with per-cube proofs small enough to keep. That is the argument for pointing the @home splitting machinery at this shape rather than waiting on a lottery ticket.
- CI builds the vendored drat-trim, which it had no way to get since `vendor/` is gitignored and runners never call `script/setup`. Proof-checking specs are tagged `:checker` and skip with a pointer to `script/setup` when the binary is absent, so a fresh clone runs the rest of the suite instead of drowning. Because skipping could otherwise turn a broken build into a green run, CI asserts the binary exists before any spec runs. Only the two coordinator specs that actually invoke the checker are tagged, so the refusals that matter most (bytes not matching their digest, a proof nobody asked for) still run everywhere.
- Fixed the `:home` skip guard, which had silently stopped guarding. It proved a coordinator database existed by building a `Store` and closing it, which worked when a store opened its connection eagerly. Pooling made connections lazy, so the guard succeeded on machines with no Postgres at all, nothing skipped, and every @home spec failed on its first real query. It now runs an actual query once and remembers the answer. This is why CI was red before drat-trim was ever the problem.
- CI runs the @home layer instead of skipping it. A Postgres service container means the coordinator, server and archiver specs execute rather than skipping for want of a database, which 49 of them had been doing. Because skipping is how this suite copes with a missing dependency, a skip in CI is a silent hole rather than a convenience, so the run now fails when anything skips for want of Postgres or drat-trim. The IPASIR specs stay exempt, since `script/build-ipasir` links a macOS `.dylib` against Homebrew's libcadical and a Linux runner cannot build it without porting the script.
- CI pins its solvers. It built kissat from upstream master, so it tested whatever the solver became today while the archive claimed `kissat 4.0.4` produced its refutations. Now pinned to `rel-4.0.4` and drat-trim to the commit the vendored copy uses, both cached on those keys so a run neither drifts nor rebuilds. Also `actions/checkout@v5` (v4 targets a deprecated Node 20), a concurrency group so a newer push cancels an older run, and read-only permissions.
- Schema: `attempts` records work that settled nothing. 9/2127's corona-3 is the first entry: `budgets.corona_depth` is now 3, and the attempt names its cost (about 3,300 core-hours as of 2026-10-06), its solvers, and that the colo pair is still running, 1,338 hours each. An `open` verdict now states its corona limit instead of leaving the reader to guess whether depth 3 was ever tried.
- The triskelion's cube-and-conquer ledgers are committed under `data/9/42947/cnf/ledgers/`: three cube files and their results, 2.9 MB, pulled home from the colo where they had sat since 2026-08-15 as the only copy of the evidence behind 9/42947 and 9/42969.
- `script/audit-cubes` checks that a cube-and-conquer campaign closes its formula: the first level is every sign pattern over one set of variables, every cube left standing at one level is partitioned by the next (a sign split or a first-true clause split), nothing is satisfiable, and the last level leaves nothing standing. `SAT::CubeLedger` reads a cube-solve ledger and `SAT::CubeCover` does the partition checks. The triskelion passes: 4,096 sign cubes, 6 standing, each split 19 ways, 106 of those standing, each split 47 ways, all 8,225 refuted. The staircase fails, as its records already say.
- Schema: heesch certificates carry `refutation_kind` (`proof` or `ledger`), `provenance` (`computed` or `derived`), and `derived_from`. Six non-tilers used to distinguish their four independent computations by prose alone, legible only because a mirrored record's `refutation` string happened to start with the word "mirror". `script/verify` now asserts that a derived record names its mirror twin, and that the twin computed evidence of the same kind, checksum, and Heesch number. `script/corona` and `script/propagate-mirrors` write the fields.
- `Census::VERSION` is 0.2.0. It had been 0.1.0 since the first commit, so every one of 56,455 records credits the same version no matter which pipeline stamped it. From here on a stamp's version means something. Old records are not rewritten, since git history already says when each was touched.
- `script/check-proof` runs drat-trim on a non-tiler's refutation and stamps `certificate.checked` with the checker's commit, the date, the seconds, and the verdict. It refuses to run unless the proof bytes hash to the record's `refutation_sha256`, and stamps nothing on any verdict but VERIFIED. 8/1309 checks in 24 s and 9/48258 in 268 s. Until now a record could only say an artifact existed, not that anyone had checked it.
- 8/1309's corona-2 refutation re-solved with kissat 4.0.4, replacing the July proof whose solver version was never recorded. The formula came out byte-identical, kissat refuted it in 4.6 s, and drat-trim verified the new 12.0 MB proof in 24 s (78,782 core lemmas). The record names the new checksum and keeps the old one in `solved_with`. The July proof was kept beside the new one until the new one checked, then deleted. It remains in git history.
